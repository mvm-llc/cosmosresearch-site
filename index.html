Common situations

Retention is slipping.

Customers are leaving, and the team has tried to address it, but retention still isn't moving. I investigate what's changing for customers, what the company has already tried, and where product or customer issues may be contributing.

Growth isn't meeting the plan.

The business is investing in its product, but growth isn't following. I examine the assumptions connecting those investments to the result you expect, including what customers need, use, and are willing to pay for.

A product investment is on the table.

A build or major change is being considered, but leadership needs to know whether it addresses the right problem. I assess the customer need, the evidence behind the proposed investment, and the alternatives before more resources are committed.

These are starting points. Every investigation begins with the specific question your team is facing.

How I work

I can come in once there's a clear concern, or earlier, when the team needs help deciding what to investigate.

The questions I'm asking: What does the business need to achieve? What do customers actually depend on? What has the team already tried? And what evidence connects the proposed work to the result everyone is working toward?

Depending on the question, that may mean reviewing available product and customer data, speaking with team members and customers, examining the reasoning behind a plan, and modeling potential business outcomes. You'll leave with a clearer view of what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain, and what leadership can do next.

I take on the investigation without putting the full burden on an already stretched team.

FAQ

When should I bring you in?
When a business result matters, but the path to improving it isn't clear. You may already know there's a retention, growth, or product issue, or you may have a concern and need help figuring out what's worth investigating.
What if we're not sure there's a problem yet?
That's a fine time to reach out. I can come in when a concern is emerging, not just after it's obvious. Sometimes the investigation is figuring out whether there's something to investigate.
What do you need to get started?
A defined question or concern, access to relevant information, and time with the people closest to the problem. We'll agree on the scope and access needed before the engagement begins. The initial conversation is separate from the paid working session.
Do you only work with product teams?
No. I work with leadership and the teams connected to the question: product, engineering, customer-facing teams, and others involved in the business decision.
Will you stay involved after the investigation?
When that's part of the engagement. The longer engagement includes continued advising as the team works through the findings and decides how to act.
